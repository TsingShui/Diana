The old get-class result exposes three methods. The marker returns the expected string. Reading c shows the left rotation feeds a.a along with the original divisor, then Integer.rotateRight consumes the helper result. Reading the old a confirms that Integer.reverse precedes division, its signed quotient goes to Math.abs, and the addition of 99 precedes the return. The two nonzero-divisor examples in the fixture exercise the zero and negative quotient cases.

The new get-class result has two methods. Its marker still returns the same string. Its a nests the left rotation in Integer.reverse, performs the division before calling Math.abs, and sends the adjusted result to the right rotation. The new a thus contains the old caller's two surrounding operations and its helper's internal computation. Division by zero skips the call to Math.abs and all remaining arithmetic in both versions. The corresponding expressions also preserve Java int overflow behavior of the integral operations.

These facts are independently consistent with the actual decompiled paths. This was a semantic review, without Android execution.
